About Pole Annotation

AI that automates the photo-annotation half of the make-ready process.

Why make-ready needs this

Before a new fiber or cable attacher can go on a utility pole, every existing attachment must be measured and inventoried — the make-ready survey. Today that means a field tech photographs each pole and span, and an annotator spends minutes per photo: calibrating heights against a survey ruler, marking every insulator and equipment point, classifying its hardware and cable type, and tracing which wire goes where across the span.

Pole Annotation does that annotation pass automatically. Photos go in; a reviewer gets a pre-filled pole record to confirm instead of a blank one to build.

What it automates

  • Calibrate — finds the survey ruler and its tick marks, fits a projective height model, and cross-checks every anchor point against the model (pass / warn / fail).
  • Measure — stamps a calibrated height on the pole top, every attachment, and every piece of equipment (transformers, street lights, risers, drip loops).
  • Classify — names the hardware (pin, spool, three-bolt, deadend, crossarm ×K…) and its cable class (primary, neutral, secondary, CATV, telco, fiber…).
  • Trace — matches attachments across pole → midspan → pole so each conductor is one record, not three disconnected marks.

The output mirrors the structure annotation tools expect, so a human reviews and fixes rather than draws from scratch.
